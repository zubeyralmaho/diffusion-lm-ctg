\begin{abstract}
We study controllable text generation with continuous-space diffusion language
models and compare them against autoregressive baselines on the E2E NLG
benchmark. Our implementation of Diffusion-LM uses prefix conditioning, where
the meaning representation occupies a clean prefix and only target positions
are diffused; prefix embeddings are clamped at every denoising step, enforcing
attribute control by construction. We compare against fine-tuned GPT-2 and T5
baselines along three axes: fluency (BLEU, ROUGE-L), lexical diversity
(distinct-$n$), and controllability (slot-fill accuracy). We further conduct
ablations over noise schedule (sqrt vs. cosine), DDIM sampling steps, and
prefix length. Results indicate that diffusion-based generation trades a
modest BLEU drop for substantial gains in lexical diversity while maintaining
competitive slot fidelity.

\keywords{Diffusion models \and Controllable generation \and Non-autoregressive
generation \and E2E NLG \and Natural language generation.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Autoregressive (AR) language models dominate modern text generation, but their
sequential decoding limits flexibility for fine-grained attribute control and
tends to under-explore the output distribution, hurting lexical diversity.
Diffusion models, which have transformed image synthesis, offer a
non-autoregressive alternative that operates in a continuous embedding space
and supports plug-and-play guidance \cite{li2022diffusionlm}. This project
implements Diffusion-LM with prefix conditioning on the E2E NLG benchmark
\cite{novikova2017e2e} and asks: \emph{can diffusion-based text generation
match autoregressive fluency while improving controllability and diversity?}

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A faithful Diffusion-LM implementation with three interchangeable noise
        schedules and prefix-clamped DDIM sampling.
  \item Side-by-side comparison with two strong AR baselines (GPT-2 and T5)
        on E2E NLG using fluency, diversity, and controllability metrics.
  \item Ablation studies isolating the effect of noise schedule, sampling
        steps, and prefix length.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Diffusion language models.}
Diffusion-LM \cite{li2022diffusionlm} introduced continuous-space text
diffusion with a learned embedding lookup and a transformer denoiser. SSD-LM
and DiffuSeq extended the idea to sequence-to-sequence settings.

\paragraph{Controllable generation.}
Plug-and-Play Language Models, PPLM, FUDGE, and classifier-guided diffusion
all impose attribute constraints at decoding. Our prefix-clamping approach is
closest to inpainting in vision diffusion.

\paragraph{E2E NLG benchmark.}
E2E NLG \cite{novikova2017e2e} pairs restaurant meaning representations (MRs)
with natural language descriptions. The cleaned version we use addresses
slot-noise issues in the original release.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Diffusion-LM with prefix conditioning.}
Token ids $w_{1:L}$ are embedded as $x_0 = E[w_{1:L}] \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times d}$.
The forward process injects Gaussian noise according to a chosen schedule:
\begin{equation}
  q(x_t \mid x_0) = \mathcal{N}(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\, x_0,\, (1-\bar\alpha_t)\,I).
\end{equation}
We use the sqrt schedule of \cite{li2022diffusionlm}:
$\bar\alpha_t = 1 - \sqrt{t/T + s}$. A transformer denoiser
$f_\theta(x_t, t)$ is trained to predict $x_0$.

\textbf{Prefix conditioning.} The first $P$ positions hold the tokenized MR.
We keep these positions at $x_0$ in the forward process (no noise) and exclude
them from the loss. At sampling time we clamp the prefix embeddings at every
DDIM step, so the denoised target is conditioned on the MR throughout.

\paragraph{Loss.}
We combine the embedding-space MSE with an auxiliary rounding cross-entropy
on the target positions only:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}
  = \mathbb{E}_{t,\,x_0,\,\epsilon}\!\left[
      \mathbf{1}_{\text{tgt}} \odot \big( \|x_0 - f_\theta(x_t,t)\|^2
      + \text{CE}(W^\top f_\theta(x_t,t),\, w) \big)
    \right].
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Baselines.}
\textbf{GPT-2} (small): causal LM fine-tuned with input
``MR \texttt{<|sep|>} reference''. \textbf{T5-small}: seq2seq with source=MR,
target=reference.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Dataset.} E2E NLG cleaned \cite{novikova2017e2e}; 33{,}525 / 4{,}299
/ 4{,}693 train/val/test pairs. MR slots: \texttt{name}, \texttt{eatType},
\texttt{food}, \texttt{priceRange}, \texttt{customerRating}, \texttt{area},
\texttt{familyFriendly}, \texttt{near}.

\paragraph{Training.} All models trained on a single T4 GPU (Google Colab).
Diffusion-LM: 20 epochs, batch 64, AdamW, lr $1\!\times\!10^{-4}$, 2000
diffusion steps. Baselines: 5 epochs, batch 32; lr $5\!\times\!10^{-5}$ (GPT-2)
and $3\!\times\!10^{-4}$ (T5).

\paragraph{Decoding.} GPT-2: nucleus sampling ($p\!=\!0.95$, $\tau\!=\!1.0$).
T5: beam search ($b\!=\!4$). Diffusion-LM: DDIM, 200 steps.

\paragraph{Metrics.} BLEU and ROUGE-L for fluency; distinct-1/2 for lexical
diversity; slot accuracy (fraction of MR slot values appearing verbatim in the
output) for controllability.

\section{Error Analysis}
\label{sec:errors}

\paragraph{Slot-level failure modes.} Across all models, the \texttt{food}
and \texttt{near} slots had the highest miss rates, often because the model
paraphrased or omitted them entirely. T5 was most faithful to slots but
produced the lowest distinct-$n$. Diffusion-LM occasionally generated
ungrammatical short outputs at low DDIM step counts.

\paragraph{Qualitative cases.} See \texttt{notebooks/error\_analysis.ipynb}
for per-example worst-case listings. A representative pattern: when the MR
contained both \texttt{priceRange[high]} and \texttt{customerRating[low]},
GPT-2 frequently rationalized the contradiction with hedging language, while
Diffusion-LM produced flatter descriptions that preserved both attributes.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The diversity-fluency tradeoff is clear: GPT-2 with nucleus sampling produces
the most lexically varied outputs but at a small cost in BLEU; T5 with beam
search is the most fluent but most repetitive; Diffusion-LM sits between them
on fluency while approaching T5 on slot accuracy thanks to prefix clamping.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Our Diffusion-LM uses a 6-layer transformer denoiser, which is smaller than
the original; results would likely improve with scale. Prefix clamping is a
form of \emph{hard} conditioning — softer classifier-guided variants
\cite{li2022diffusionlm} could improve fluency further.

\paragraph{Ethical Considerations.}
Generative models can hallucinate factual content and amplify biases present
in training data. On E2E the risk is bounded (restaurant descriptions), but
the diffusion-LM framework is general: deployed at scale on open-domain text
it would require bias auditing and content filtering. We do not release a
trained checkpoint with this work.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented an implementation and empirical study of prefix-conditioned
Diffusion-LM for controllable text generation on E2E NLG. The model achieves
competitive controllability with substantially improved diversity over a T5
beam-search baseline. Future work: classifier-guided control over individual
slot tokens, and cross-dataset evaluation on CommonGen.
