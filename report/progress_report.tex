\section{Project Title, Group Members, and Problem Definition}

\textbf{Title.} \emph{Controllable Text Generation via Diffusion Models}
(Term Project Topic \#4).

\textbf{Group members.} Zübeyr Almaho (300201023) --- single-member group.

\textbf{Task and motivation.} The project addresses
\textbf{data-to-text generation with attribute control}: given a structured
\emph{meaning representation} (MR) listing restaurant attributes (e.g.,
\texttt{name[Loch~Fyne], food[Japanese], priceRange[high],
familyFriendly[no]}), the system must produce a fluent natural-language
description that faithfully realizes every requested attribute. This is a
\emph{conditional natural-language generation} task. The problem is important
because controllable generation is a recurring bottleneck for safe deployment
of large language models in product descriptions, dialog responses,
report generation, and accessibility tooling: practitioners need outputs that
remain faithful to a structured intent rather than drifting into hallucination
or omitting required content.

\textbf{Input / output.} Input: a string of slot--value pairs (the MR).
Output: a single English sentence (or short paragraph) realizing all slots.

\textbf{Task type.} \emph{Generation} (conditional, data-to-text).

\textbf{Why technically challenging.}
Autoregressive (AR) decoders tend to (i)~under-explore the output distribution
when forced through likelihood-maximizing decoding such as beam search, hurting
\emph{lexical diversity}, and (ii)~silently drop or paraphrase slots when no
explicit constraint enforces them, hurting \emph{controllability}. Diffusion
language models operate in a continuous embedding space and admit
plug-and-play attribute control through inpainting or classifier guidance, but
training them stably for short, structured text is non-trivial: schedule
choice, rounding, sampling step count, and conditioning all interact.

\textbf{Topic / title change.} None since the proposal stage.

\textbf{Input format.} Eight slot types: \texttt{name}, \texttt{eatType},

\textbf{Preprocessing completed.}

(iii)~prompt flattening;

$P\!=\!32$ for the MR, target length $L-P\!=\!64$).

which we will note in the error analysis section of the final paper.

experiments. The data loader is exercised by an automated smoke test

\section{Literature Review Progress}

We reviewed three threads of work and identified the methods that ground our
choices of baseline, primary method, and evaluation.

\textbf{(1)~Diffusion language models.}
Li et al.~(\textit{Diffusion-LM}, NeurIPS~2022) introduced continuous-space
text diffusion with a learned token embedding lookup and a transformer
denoiser trained with x0-prediction plus a rounding cross-entropy. Their
``sqrt'' noise schedule and plug-and-play classifier guidance are central to
our design. Earlier image-domain work --- DDPM (Ho et al.\ 2020), improved
DDPM (Nichol \& Dhariwal 2021), and DDIM (Song et al.\ 2021) --- provides the
underlying forward process, the cosine schedule we use as an ablation, and the
deterministic accelerated sampler we adopt at inference.

\textbf{(2)~Autoregressive conditional generation.}
GPT-2 (Radford et al.\ 2019) and T5 (Raffel et al.\ 2020) are the standard AR
references for the E2E benchmark. Most published E2E systems are encoder--
decoder models or fine-tuned causal LMs; we therefore adopt one of each
(GPT-2-small and T5-small) as baselines.

\textbf{(3)~Controllable generation and evaluation.}
PPLM (Dathathri et al.\ 2020) and FUDGE establish gradient-based attribute
control over AR decoders, motivating our comparison axis of
\emph{controllability} (slot accuracy). For evaluation we follow standard E2E
practice: BLEU (Papineni et al.\ 2002) and ROUGE-L (Lin 2004) for fluency,
distinct-$n$ (Li et al.\ 2016) for lexical diversity, and slot-fill accuracy
for controllability.

\textbf{Direction implied by the review.}
The literature supports our design: a Diffusion-LM-style continuous-text
diffusion model with prefix conditioning is a faithful, well-motivated
implementation of the topic, and the chosen baselines and metrics are the
canonical comparisons for the E2E benchmark.

\section{Baseline Models or Prompting Strategies}

The project compares two AR baselines against the proposed
\emph{prefix-conditioned Diffusion-LM}. Both baselines are fully implemented
end-to-end (training, generation, and evaluation).

\textbf{Baseline~1 --- GPT-2 (124M).}
Causal LM fine-tuning. Input format: \texttt{"<MR> <|sep|> <reference><eos>"};
labels equal inputs (standard causal-LM objective). Decoding: nucleus sampling
($p\!=\!0.95$, $\tau\!=\!1.0$). \emph{Why suitable:} GPT-2 is the canonical
decoder-only AR baseline for E2E NLG; nucleus sampling is the standard
diversity-oriented decoding choice, giving the strongest possible
\emph{distinct-$n$} comparison for diffusion to match.

\textbf{Baseline~2 --- T5-small (60M).}
Encoder--decoder seq2seq with source = MR text, target = reference.
Decoding: beam search ($b\!=\!4$). \emph{Why suitable:} T5 represents the
fluency-oriented AR family that traditionally dominates E2E leaderboards and
provides the strongest \emph{BLEU} reference point.

\textbf{Implementation status.}
GPT-2 and T5 training pipelines are implemented with HuggingFace
\texttt{Trainer} / \texttt{Seq2SeqTrainer} respectively, including per-epoch
checkpointing, validation evaluation, and predict-with-generate. The training
configurations are versioned at \texttt{configs/gpt2\_baseline.yaml} and
\texttt{configs/t5\_baseline.yaml}.

\textbf{Issues encountered.}
\emph{Compute.} Local Mac hardware is insufficient for the diffusion run; we
moved training to Colab Pro on an A100, which fits the full pipeline in one
session. \emph{Library compatibility.} HuggingFace \texttt{transformers}
recently renamed \texttt{evaluation\_strategy} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{eval\_strategy};
the codebase targets $\geq$4.40 to remain forward-compatible.
\emph{API.} No paid API is used; everything runs locally / on Colab.

\section{Planned Improvements and Technical Direction}

Beyond the current functional baseline of the diffusion model, we have
identified four concrete improvements with high expected impact and tractable
implementation cost:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Self-conditioning} (Chen et al.\ 2022). Feed the previous
        step's $\hat x_0$ prediction as auxiliary input to the next step.
        Expected: $\sim$+1--2 BLEU at no extra inference cost.
  \item \textbf{EMA of denoiser weights.} Standard practice in diffusion
        training; expected: $\sim$+0.5--1 BLEU and visibly smoother samples.
  \item \textbf{Clamping trick at inference.} Periodically snap $\hat x_0$ to
        the nearest token embedding during sampling, as in the original
        Diffusion-LM paper; expected to improve both fluency and slot
        accuracy.
  \item \textbf{Batched diffusion sampling.} Replace the current
        \texttt{batch\_size=1} sampler with a batched version
        ($\sim$10$\times$ speedup), which makes the full ablation suite
        feasible inside a single Colab session.
\end{enumerate}

A stretch goal (treated as a graduate-level extension, listed as
\emph{future work} in the final paper if time does not allow) is to swap
hard prefix clamping for \emph{soft} classifier guidance: train per-slot
classifiers on noisy embeddings and inject their gradients during sampling.

\section{Error Analysis and Generation Quality Analysis}

A dedicated Jupyter notebook (\texttt{notebooks/error\_analysis.ipynb}) is
already in place and will populate the final report. It computes:
(i)~per-slot \emph{miss rate} (which attributes models tend to drop or
paraphrase); (ii)~output-length histograms per model; (iii)~a
diversity-vs-fluency scatter (distinct-2 against BLEU); (iv)~the five
worst-case per-example dumps for every model, ranked by per-example slot
recall.

For the generation-quality discussion we will examine: \emph{fluency} (does
the model produce grammatical English?), \emph{coherence} (does the
sentence read as a unit?), \emph{slot fidelity} (are all MR values
present?), \emph{repetition} (is there token-level or phrasal repetition?
T5 with beam search is particularly susceptible), and \emph{hallucination}
(does the model invent attributes not in the MR? Free-form AR sampling is
the typical offender). Representative cases for each failure mode will be
extracted from the test set for the qualitative section of the LNCS paper.

\section{Ethical Considerations and Bias}

The project is a generative model and therefore inherits the standard set of
responsible-AI concerns, in moderated form because the application domain
(restaurant descriptions) carries low real-world harm potential.

\textbf{Hallucination.} AR baselines can invent attributes not in the MR;
prefix clamping bounds this risk for the diffusion model but does not
eliminate it in the free-form target region.

\textbf{Dataset bias.} E2E~NLG is English-only, restaurant-specific, and
United-Kingdom-centric; conclusions do not transfer cleanly to other
languages or domains.

\textbf{Evaluation limits.} BLEU, ROUGE, and distinct-$n$ are surface metrics
and correlate imperfectly with human judgement of fluency. Our slot-accuracy
metric is currently a strict substring match and will under-credit
paraphrased attribute realizations; we plan to optionally integrate the
official E2E scorer in the final paper as a sanity check.

\textbf{Release.} No model checkpoints will be released. The codebase is
public to support reproducibility but the trained weights stay private to
the project, sidestepping any misuse pathway.
